# Stewardship Doctrine for Entity Sovereignty

## Purpose

This document extends Identity Is Prime from citizen-first identity protection into a broader stewardship doctrine.

Identity Is Prime already treats human identity as a decomposition of protected prime topics rather than a flat profile. This doctrine adds a missing civilizational relation: stewardship.

Most identity systems model ownership, authentication, and permissions. A humane identity system must also model responsibility across time.

The guiding thesis is:

A person is not reducible to an account, profile, dataset, credential, wallet, ship, score, twin, or model. Every representation is partial. The living human remains prior to every digital representation.

## Relationship categories

The platform must distinguish at least four relationship categories.

Identity relationships describe what an entity is.

Trust relationships describe confidence, testimony, or reliance between entities.

Capability relationships describe what an entity may do under bounded authority.

Stewardship relationships describe what an entity is responsible for preserving, teaching, caring for, maintaining, reviewing, or handing forward.

The fourth category is the missing primitive.

Ownership says: this is mine.

Stewardship says: this is entrusted to me.

A platform that collapses stewardship into ownership becomes coercive. A platform that ignores stewardship becomes forgetful.

## Brahmarshi design law

The Brahmarshi way is the path of the keeper rather than the possessor. In platform terms, this means the system must increase truthfulness, agency, responsibility, and continuity without turning the person into an administrative object.

The platform must obey these laws.

First, identity is continuity, not possession.

Second, stewardship is higher than ownership.

Third, agents must increase human agency rather than dependency.

Fourth, claims must carry provenance.

Fifth, consent must be active, revocable, scoped, and legible.

Sixth, succession must be designed before crisis.

Seventh, the system must protect against itself.

Eighth, truth and compassion must remain coupled.

## Non-reduction rule

The following are prohibited reductions.

A person is not their account.

A person is not their device.

A person is not their digital twin.

A person is not their projection.

A person is not their capability score.

A person is not their employment graph.

A person is not an inferred profile.

A person is not an agent's summary of them.

Every projection must remain explicitly partial.

## Prime-topic preservation

Identity Is Prime protects the person by preventing unsafe multiplication of identity topics across contexts.

Stewardship adds a second invariant.

A steward may be responsible for a topic, artifact, memory, relationship, or obligation without owning the person or entity represented by it.

For example, a guardian may be keeper of a child's educational record without owning the child.

A maintainer may be keeper of a repository without owning every contributor's identity.

A family member may be keeper of a memory artifact without owning the family members named inside it.

A research agent may be keeper of a claim trail without owning the claim's truth.

## Required proof posture

Any system that emits identity-affecting or stewardship-affecting artifacts must preserve evidence sufficient to answer these questions.

Who asserted this?

Under what authority?

From what evidence?

With what scope?

With what consent?

For what purpose?

For how long?

Who is keeper?

Who inherits responsibility if the keeper fails?

What would falsify or revoke the claim?

## Repository role

This repository remains the reference implementation for citizen-first identity, prime-topic protection, policy vetoes, and proof artifacts.

The stewardship extension should be implemented here as doctrine, tests, and toy examples before being promoted into larger graph and twin runtimes.
